How the scan works

Universe. Every US-listed stock (NASDAQ, NYSE, AMEX), then only tradeable ones: price over $1, at least 150k shares and $1M traded a day, market cap $50M to $50B. Mega caps are left out on purpose.

A setup needs all of these:

  • The move: the top of the base is at least 30% above the lowest price of the 60 days before it.
  • The base: 5 to 40 days moving sideways in a range no deeper than 18%, near the stock's recent highs.
  • Getting tighter: the second half of the base swings less than the first half, and its lows are higher.
  • Trend intact: the 20-day EMA is above the 50-day EMA and price is near or above the 20-day.
  • Moves enough to matter: average daily range (ADR) of at least 2.5%. This removes stocks pinned by a takeover offer.

Stages. Base forming: inside the base. Near breakout: within 3% of the top, or above it on light volume. Breakout: above the top of the base (no more than 10% above) with volume at least 1.3× the 20-day average. During market hours, volume is projected to a full day, so early readings can overstate it.

Plan shown on breakouts. Entry at the current price, stop at today's low. If that stop is further away than the stock's ADR, the card warns you, because the risk is too wide.

Score (0–100) ranks setups within a stage: tighter base, bigger prior move, volume drying up in the base, and closeness to the breakout level.

This is a screen, not advice. In a backtest of similar rules on about 1,000 US stocks over two years, only about a third of breakouts worked; the profits came from a few big winners.
